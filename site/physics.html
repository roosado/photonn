Scalar diffraction, and where it stops working

Everything rests on moving a field from one plane to the next

Before anything can be trained, coherent light has to be moved across 3 mm of air exactly — and the simulation has to know when it can no longer do that. One method does the moving, one criterion marks the edge, and one structural fact caps what the whole idea can ever compute.

The propagator

Decompose into plane waves, delay each one, add them back up

A field is decomposed into plane waves by a Fourier transform, each is advanced by its own axial phase, and they are recombined. That is the whole angular-spectrum method: transform, multiply by H = exp(i 2πz √(1/λ² − f²)), transform back. It makes no paraxial approximation — it is exact up to the grid’s band limit, which is why Fresnel, Fraunhofer and the differentiable PyTorch layer are all checked against it rather than the other way round. Against an analytic Gaussian beam it agrees to ~2×10−8.

Two behaviours fall straight out of that square root. Where f² > 1/λ² it turns imaginary, and computing it as a complex sqrt makes those components decay with distance — evanescent waves, for free, with no special case. And for large z the transfer function oscillates faster than the frequency grid can sample, which is aliasing. Zeroing H beyond the local-frequency limit keeps the method alias-free into the far field, at the cost of discarding content past the propagating band.

The edge is a single criterion, z_crit = N·dx²/λ — 15.4 mm at this operating point, against 3 mm hops. It is enforced at runtime, not only in tests. Note what it does not contain: z_crit depends on λ, dx and N but not on the aperture — which is why, below, the aperture control does not move the sampling threshold no matter how far you drag it.

Live — the diffraction runs on a hand-written FFT in your browser, the same physics as the Python reference (cross-checked to <10−6). Move any control; the sampling flag turns amber the moment z > z_crit.

Drawing it without lying about it

Why the 3D stack has haze in it and no rays

The light drawn between the mask planes on the front page is not decoration. A hop can be split into sub-hops and re-run because H(z₁)·H(z₂) = H(z₁+z₂), and the one z-dependent term that would break that equality — the band limit above — is inactive below z_crit. At 3 mm against 15.4 mm it is inactive, so the intermediate planes are exact fields, not interpolation. Above z_crit the equality genuinely breaks, and the test suite asserts both halves of that.

No rays are drawn, deliberately. Scalar diffraction is not ray optics, and straight lines from digit to detector would misrepresent the one thing the figure exists to show — that every point of the input is reaching every detector at once. The stack is 18 mm long across a 1.02 mm aperture, about 18:1, so drawn to scale it is an unreadable needle; the depth axis is compressed and the figure states its own compression factor on its face rather than quietly flattering the geometry.

The drawing also cannot touch the answer. The prediction still comes from the canonical six propagations, and the cross-check asserts the logits are bit-identical with slicing switched on.

The limit, stated precisely

Optical depth is not depth in the machine-learning sense

Propagation is linear in the field. A phase mask is linear in the field. Their composition is therefore a single linear operator:

E_out = M · E_in, with M = P_L D_L P_{L−1} D_{L−1} … D₁ P₀ — each P a propagation, each D a diagonal mask.

No matter how many mask-and-propagation layers are stacked, the field-to-field map collapses to one linear map. Extra layers add parameters and let M better approximate a desired operator under the physical constraint that it factorise into phase-only masks separated by diffraction — but they add no nonlinear compositional power whatsoever. Stacking here is not stacking in the sense the phrase normally carries.

The only nonlinearity is the detection itself. The logit for class c is the intensity summed over its detector region, which works out to s_c = E_in† A_c E_in with A_c = M† R_c M ≻ 0 — a positive-semidefinite quadratic form in the input field. So the classifier computes one PSD quadratic form per class and takes the argmax. That is strictly more than a linear classifier, and it is exactly one nonlinear layer: linear optical transform, magnitude-square, linear sum.

One consequence is worth pulling out, because it is the only place a nonlinearity touches the raw pixels: the input encoding. Writing the image into phase applies a nonlinear exp(i·π·image) map before the optics ever sees it, which is why the encoding choice measurably changes achievable accuracy. It is doing nonlinear work the linear optics cannot.

Per the project’s scope this limit is characterised, not engineered around. No physical activation functions, no nonlinear media, no larger electronic head. If a bigger electronic head would lift accuracy, that is a finding to report, not a bug to fix.

Verified against analytics

Angular spectrum against an analytic Gaussian to ~2×10−8; Fresnel against ASM in its validity range to ~9×10−9; Fraunhofer of a circular aperture against the Airy pattern, first null on the predicted ring.

Enforced, not assumed

Sampling is a runtime check, not just a test: check_sampling returns a report and the explorer flags the crossing live. Beyond z_crit the method does not fail loudly — which is precisely why it is flagged.

Scope

Scalar theory only: no vector or polarisation-resolved propagation, and no full-wave solver. If real component data is ever wanted it gets imported as measurements, not simulated here.
