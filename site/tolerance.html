The question the project exists to answer

How precisely must a photonic chip be built before it stops computing what it was trained to compute?

Everything so far was ideal. The masks were exact, the planes were exactly 3 mm apart, and every pixel took exactly the phase it was trained to take. Now the trained parameters cross into an independent as-built model and get broken on purpose, one imperfection at a time, until the number that decides feasibility falls out.

Method

Now break it on purpose

The trained parameters cross a one-directional handoff — a single HDF5 file carrying the phase masks, the geometry, the operating point and the frozen test set — into a MATLAB “as-built” model that never writes back. That boundary is the design; it keeps the ideal network and the imperfect one from contaminating each other. Nothing is retrained on the far side, and no error model exists on the near side.

That model then re-scores the exact same network under each fabrication imperfection in turn — per-pixel phase error, DAC quantisation, optical loss, wavelength drift, thermal crosstalk, detector and shot noise — Monte Carlo over realisations with the seeds recorded, every magnitude traced to a published measurement and cited inline at the point of use.

One control makes it a measurement rather than a demonstration: with zero error injected the as-built model reproduces 0.7990 exactly, the same number the PyTorch model scores on the same 2,000 images. Two independent implementations agreeing to the last digit means any drop below it is fabrication and nothing else. That anchor has held through every change in the project.

The bar throughout is 95% of ideal accuracy, which is ≥ 0.7591. Edges are quoted as the bracket the sweep actually resolves — holds at X, fails at Y — rather than interpolated between grid points, so they stay comparable across models.

Binding constraint

Thermal / pixel crosstalk sets the tolerance, and it is the one that fails. Accuracy holds while the phase blur between neighbouring pixels stays at 0.25 px and fails by 0.5 px; three quarters of a pixel of blur collapses the network to near chance. A standard LCoS modulator’s fringing-field crosstalk is about 1 px — so this design, as specified, would not work on one. The ranking is unambiguous: crosstalk » phase error > detector power ≈ loss » wavelength ≈ quantisation. To hold ≥ 0.7591: phase σ ≤ 0.3 rad (λ/21), ≥3-bit DAC, drift ≤ 10 nm, and ≥1 pW over 1 ms. The number that decides feasibility is not precision or bit depth — it is how sharply each pixel’s phase is confined against its neighbours.

Read the ranking rather than the individual numbers. Phase calibration and bit depth are comfortable: a well-calibrated modulator sits at λ/100 ≈ 0.05 rad, an order of magnitude inside the 0.3 rad that still holds, and 3-bit control suffices against an 8-bit standard. Wavelength is a non-issue for any temperature-controlled source. Detection is nine orders of magnitude from shot-noise-limited at the nominal operating point.

Loss is the interesting one, because it does not act alone. Uniform attenuation cancels exactly in the power-normalised readout, so when photons are plentiful it has zero effect on accuracy. It only bites through the photon budget — which is why it is swept at the shot-noise knee, where every 3 dB halves the photon count. Loss and the power budget have to be reasoned about together or the sweep measures nothing.

And a better-trained network was not a more fragile one, which is worth stating because the opposite is a reasonable prior. Retraining on five times more data lifted the ideal baseline from 0.7695 to 0.7990 and every edge was re-measured against a correspondingly stricter bar. Five of the six landed in the same bracket. Masks fitted to more data did not carry finer, more brittle structure — but that result belongs to training, and the next section is what happened when the optics changed instead.

The result this project actually has

What depth costs

A deeper network is a lot more accurate. Spending the same diffractive reach budget on 56 masks instead of 5 reaches 0.9040 on the same frozen test set, against 0.7990 — and it is running live, beside the shipped model → so the improvement is easy to see and easy to mistake for a straightforward upgrade.

It is not one. The whole budget above was re-run against that candidate, scored against its own correspondingly stricter bar of 0.8588, and +10.5 points of accuracy is priced in fabrication tolerance:

Three readings are worth separating. The binding constraint does not move at all — crosstalk fails at the same 0.25 px edge, and a real modulator’s ~1 px fringing field destroys either design. Depth neither helps nor hurts the thing that already made this unbuildable.

The two sources that loosened both follow from photon capture. The deep stack routes 79.1% of input photons into the detector boxes against ~60% for the shipped design — the same “route rather than scatter” mechanism the accuracy gain comes from. Better signal at the readout drops the shot-noise knee a decade and buys wavelength margin. The design became more robust exactly where it already passed by nine orders of magnitude.

Loss points opposite ways in its two units, and the per-element one governs. In total the candidate tolerates more attenuation — 12 dB against 5 — but that larger budget is divided among eleven times more elements, so the per-mask requirement tightens to 0.214 dB. A datasheet quotes per element, and 0.214 dB/mask sits at the optimistic end of the realistic 0.2–1 dB range. Loss moves from comfortable to marginal.

Finding · depth adds a constraint without relieving one

Ten and a half points of accuracy cost 2× tighter phase control, one more DAC bit and 4.7× tighter loss per element, while the source that already fails against real hardware does not move. This is why the deep model is labelled “not shipped” wherever it appears: its number is real and measured exactly like the headline, but what it costs is a build tolerance this project has no evidence anyone can hit. The trade is a more useful result than a clean win would have been.

There is also something the budget cannot say. At 56 masks the plane spacing falls from 3 mm to 0.53 mm, so a ±10 µm spacing error goes from 0.33% of the gap to 1.9% — and past roughly forty plates the stack is better described as a volume of glass than as discrete phase screens, which is a different thing to fabricate. This budget covers device errors only and has nothing on geometry. Alignment and calibration — plane spacing, lateral registration, systematic phase gain, detector offset — plausibly bind the deep design before anything in the table does. That is flagged, not measured, and it is the next error sources to be written.

There is no candidate version of Fig 8. The sensitivity map is one panel per mask in a single row, so at 56 masks it is 16,139 pixels wide and 341 tall — 47:1, which is fifteen pixels high in a grid cell and thirty across the full column. There is no size at which it can be read, so it is left out rather than shown as a smear. It also costs 2,016 evaluations to compute against 36 for the shipped design, about four hours and most of a full run. Both of those are the same fact from different directions: past roughly forty plates, per-mask quantities stop being a thing you can look at.

Error budget · MZI mesh

Fabrication tolerance for the interferometer mesh

The same framework extends to the mesh, reactivating the two error sources unique to it: coupler imbalance (deviation from 50:50) and per-MZI insertion loss, which makes the transfer sub-unitary — a real effect there, unlike the normalised diffractive readout. The interesting comparison is the failure mode: serial phase-error accumulation down 72 MZI layers versus the diffractive net’s crosstalk-dominated per-pixel budget. Two optical computers, two different ways to lose the computation to fabrication.

The boundary

Python designs the ideal network; a single HDF5 file crosses to MATLAB, which models the as-built device and never writes back. Design versus as-built, enforced by a one-directional handoff rather than by discipline.

Every magnitude sourced

Phase error, bit depth, drift, read noise, insertion loss and fringing-field crosstalk each trace to a published measurement or datasheet, cited on the line the constant is defined. Modelling choices are listed as modelling choices.

Reproducible

Deterministic given the recorded seeds; the ideal baseline must read 0.7990 or the forward model is misaligned with the handoff. A candidate model can be scored without touching the shipped one.
